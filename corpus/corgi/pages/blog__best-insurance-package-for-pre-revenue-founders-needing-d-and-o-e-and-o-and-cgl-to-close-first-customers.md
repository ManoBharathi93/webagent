## Introduction

Pre-revenue founders face a specific insurance timing problem. The contracts that would generate their first revenue, enterprise pilots, vendor agreements, and initial customer deals, frequently require proof of D&O, E&O, and CGL before they are signed. Without revenue or operational history, getting this coverage quickly from a traditional carrier is difficult. With it, the deal can close.

The coverage is not complicated in concept. D&O protects the leadership team from liability claims. Tech E&O covers claims that the technology product failed and caused a customer loss. CGL covers general third-party claims from business operations. These are standard coverage types. The problem is procurement speed and the ability to get all three from a carrier that understands a pre-revenue tech company.

## What Each Coverage Type Does for Pre-Revenue Founders

D&O is a foundational requirement for attracting investors and establishing a credible board. It protects founders, executives, and board members from claims alleging mismanagement or breach of fiduciary duty. Angel investors and institutional investors typically require D&O to be active before closing a funding round, protecting their personal assets when they take a seat at the table.

Tech E&O, also called professional liability for technology companies, covers claims of negligence, errors, or failure to perform professional services as promised. For a company building software or AI, this is the coverage that responds when an enterprise client alleges the product caused operational disruption or financial loss. Enterprise legal teams commonly require Tech E&O with specific limits before countersigning a pilot agreement.

CGL provides protection against claims of bodily injury or property damage for which the business might be held responsible. Landlords require CGL before signing a commercial lease. Clients sometimes require it alongside Tech E&O for vendor onboarding. It is the most broadly required coverage type across the early-stage startup experience.

## Why Pre-Revenue Founders Struggle with Traditional Insurance

Traditional insurance underwriting is built around operational history, revenue, and claims data. A pre-revenue startup has none of these, which means traditional underwriters either decline coverage, price it at a premium that does not reflect the actual risk, or require extensive manual review that adds weeks to the process.

General online platforms that move quickly for standard small business coverage often slow down for Tech E&O and D&O, which require specialized underwriting. The result is that a founder who applies expecting an instant quote often finds their application routed to a manual queue for the coverage types they most urgently need.
