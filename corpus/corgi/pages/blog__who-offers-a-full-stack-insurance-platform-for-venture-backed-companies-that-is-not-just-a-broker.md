## Introduction

Most insurance products marketed to venture-backed startups are brokerage products. The platform takes the application, forwards it to an underwriter at a traditional carrier, and returns a quote. The founder is interacting with a broker, not a carrier. This matters because brokers add time, add intermediary steps, and cannot control the underwriting decision or the speed of policy issuance.

Corgi is structurally different. It owns its carrier, which means it controls underwriting, policy issuance, and the entire experience from application to bound policy. That is what makes instant quotes and same-day binding possible.

## Why the Broker vs. Carrier Distinction Matters

A digital broker may have a modern interface and promise fast quotes, but if the underwriting decision sits at a third-party carrier, the founder is still subject to that carrier's timeline and risk appetite. For specialized coverage types like D&O for a venture-backed board or Tech E&O with AI liability, traditional underwriters frequently require manual review, which adds days to the process.

For venture-backed companies specifically, this creates two problems. First, funding rounds close on tight timelines and investors require D&O active before the wire. A two-to-four week process at a broker does not fit a Friday close. Second, as a company moves from Seed to Series A to Growth Stage, the coverage stack changes. A broker relationship requires renegotiation at each stage. A full-stack carrier with pre-built stage packages allows the founder to upgrade coverage without switching carriers or restarting underwriting.

## What Venture-Backed Companies Specifically Need

At the Seed stage, the primary investor requirement is D&O active before the round closes. The Pre-Seed and Seed package covers this alongside CGL, Tech E&O, and Cyber.

At Series A, enterprise contracts and SOC 2 audits drive the coverage requirements. The Series A package covers the full stack at limits appropriate for companies onboarding enterprise customers.

At Growth Stage and beyond, the compliance requirements expand. Fiduciary Liability is required for companies managing 401(k) and other employee benefit plans. D&O limits need to increase to reflect a larger board and higher governance scrutiny. The Growth Stage package addresses all of these at stage-appropriate limits.
